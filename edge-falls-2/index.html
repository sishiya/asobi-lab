    この世界のきまりHow this world works

    • 大地は平らな円盤。水は低い方へ流れ、ふちを越えると滝になって深淵へ落ちる。落ちた水の多くは深淵にたまり、泉からまたわき出る。のこりはしぶきの霧になって空へのぼる。
    • 何も生まれず、何も消えない。土は削った分だけ盛れ、泉は深淵の水をくみ上げ、滝は深淵へ返す。去った命も深淵を通って、少しずつつぼへ戻ってくる。
    • 太陽は大地の下へは沈まず、上をぐるりと回る。太陽から遠い所が夜。日の当たる水は雲になり、雲は天といっしょにめぐって、山にぶつかると雨になる。
    • 月が上を通ると、水がそちらへ寄る（潮）。月の下のふちから、水がよけいにあふれる。月と太陽がそろうとき（新月）と向かい合うとき（満月）は潮が大きく（大潮）、直角のときは小さい（小潮）。
    • 日ざしはたまる。太陽が回っていれば大地はならされるが、止めると真下は暑くなって乾き、草も焼ける。反対がわは冷えて凍る。
    • 肥えは消えない。育つ草は土の肥えを体にとりこみ、枯れると枯れ草に、枯れ草はゆっくりくさって土にもどる。食べる者がいないと枯れ草が積もって芽をふさぎ、草はまばらなわら色になっていく。シカがほどよく食べると、ふんと、ふみつけた枯れ草で早く土にもどり、草地は青く保たれる（食べすぎれば草はなくなる）。水は肥えを下流へ運び、滝から深淵へ。泉がくみ上げる。
    • 夏は太陽の道が内がわを回って強く照らし、冬は外がわを回って弱い。山の上や夜の側は寒い。0 度より下では雨が雪になり、水は凍る（凍った水は流れず、上を歩ける）。春に雪がとける。
    • 草はうるおいと光で育つ。シカは草を、オオカミはシカを、サカナは水草を、サギは浅瀬のサカナを食べる。腹がいっぱいの大人は、暖かいとき（春〜夏）に子を残す。深い水に入ると流され、ふちまで流されると滝から落ちる。
    • The land is a flat disk. Water flows downhill, and past the edge it becomes a waterfall and drops into the Abyss. Most of the fallen water collects in the Abyss and comes up again from the springs. The rest rises into the sky as mist from the spray.
    • Nothing is made and nothing is lost. You can pile up only as much soil as you dig, springs draw up water from the Abyss, and the falls give it back. Lives that pass away also travel through the Abyss and slowly come back to the jar.
    • The Sun never sets below the land. It circles above it. Places far from the Sun have night. Sunlit water turns into clouds, the clouds circle with the sky, and when they hit a mountain, it rains.
    • When the Moon passes overhead, water gathers toward it (the tide). More water spills over the edge under the Moon. When the Moon and Sun line up (new moon) or face each other (full moon), the tides are big (spring tides); at right angles they are small (neap tides).
    • Sunlight builds up. While the Sun circles, the land evens out, but stop it and the land right under it gets hot and dry, and the grass scorches. The far side cools and freezes.
    • Richness is never lost. Growing grass takes richness from the soil into its body; when it dies it becomes dead grass, which slowly rots back into the soil. With nothing grazing, dead grass piles up and smothers new shoots, so the grass turns thin and straw-colored. When deer graze moderately, their droppings and the dead grass they trample return it to the soil quickly, and the grassland stays green (graze too much and the grass is gone). Water carries richness downstream and over the falls into the Abyss, and springs draw it back up.
    • In summer the Sun’s path runs on the inside and shines strongly. In winter it runs on the outside and is weak. Mountaintops and the night side are cold. Below 0 °C, rain turns to snow and water freezes (frozen water doesn’t flow, and animals can walk on it). Snow melts in spring.
    • Grass grows with water and light. Deer eat grass, wolves eat deer, fish eat water plants, and herons eat fish in shallow water. Well-fed adults have young when it is warm (spring to summer). Animals that go into deep water get carried away, and if they are carried to the edge, they fall off the falls.

    遺伝子と進化Genes and evolution

    • 生き物はそれぞれ3つの遺伝子を持つ。シカは「あしの速さ・体の大きさ・泳ぐ力」、オオカミは「あしの速さ・体の大きさ・ねばり強さ」、サカナは「泳ぐ力・体の大きさ・浅瀬への強さ」、サギは「つばさ・体の大きさ・くちばしの長さ」。
    • 子は親と近くの相手の遺伝子を半分ずつもらい、少しだけ変わる（突然変異）。どの遺伝子にも得と損があり、世界のきびしさに合ったものが生き残って、平均が動いていく。
    • Each animal has 3 genes. Deer: “leg speed, body size, swimming”. Wolves: “leg speed, body size, stamina”. Fish: “swimming, body size, toughness in shallow water”. Herons: “wings, body size, beak length”.
    • Young get half their genes from their parent and half from a nearby mate, with small changes (mutations). Every gene has a good side and a cost. The ones that fit how hard the world is survive, and the averages shift.

    本当は（でどころ）What is really true (sources)

    地球は丸く、果てはありません。水は重力で地球の中心へ引かれるので、どこまで行っても落ちません。川の水は海へ行き、海や陸から蒸発して雲になり、雨になってまた川へ（水の循環）。泉の水は、雨がしみこんだ地下水です。昼と夜は地球が回るから、潮は月と太陽の引く力の差で、地球の両がわがふくらみます。

    季節は、地球の自転の軸が 23.4 度かたむいたまま太陽のまわりを回るので、太陽の高さと昼の長さが変わって起きます。平らな大地の図には「夏は太陽が内がわ、冬は外がわを回る」と描くものがありますが、本当の地球ではそうなりません。寒い所のなかまほど体が大きい傾向（ベルクマンの規則）は本当に見られます（例外もたくさんあります）。

    生き残りやすい性質が子に伝わって積み重なり、生き物が変わっていく考え（自然選択）は、ダーウィンとウォレスが 1858 年に発表しました。本当の進化はふつう何百〜何万世代もかかります。この箱庭では、数十世代で目に見えるように、ずっと速くしています。

    大地を平らな円盤と考えた人は、昔たくさんいました。古代ギリシャのホメロスの詩では、平らな大地のまわりを大きな川オケアノスが取り巻き、タレスは大地が水に浮かぶと考えました。古代メソポタミアでは、大地の下に真水の海（アプスー）があり、泉や川の水はそこから来ると考えられていました。でも、世界の果てから水が落ちる滝は、近ごろの絵や物語でよく描かれる姿で、昔の人がそう書き残した例はあまり知られていません。「中世の人はみな大地が平らだと信じていた」もよくある誤解で、学のある人は大地が丸いと知っていました（天動説のプトレマイオスも、大地は丸いと考えていました）。

    本当の潮も、新月と満月のころに大きくなります（大潮）。月と太陽の引く力が同じ線の上で重なるからです。多くの動物には決まった活動の時間があり、オオカミは夕方から夜によく狩りをします。クサフグやサンゴのように、月の満ち欠けに合わせて卵を産む生き物も本当にいます。捕食者は、遅いもの・弱いものをつかまえやすく、これが獲物の逃げる力を進化させる力の一つになります。

    同じ種の群れが、山や海で長く隔てられると、それぞれの場所に合った形に変わり、やがて出会っても子を残さない別の種になります（地理的隔離による種分化）。本当はふつう何千〜何万年もかかります。この箱庭ではずっと速くしています。

    死んだ生き物やふんは、小さな生き物（分解者）に分けられて、炭素や窒素などにもどり、また植物の体になります。体をつくる物は、こうしてめぐっています（物質の循環）。でも、命が光になって雲にのり、つぼへ帰る、というのはこの箱庭の作りごとです。遺伝子は、親から子へしか伝わりません。

    年の頁には、年ごとの「本当は」が書きこまれていく。

    The Earth is round and has no edge. Gravity pulls water toward the center of the Earth, so it never falls off, however far it goes. River water flows to the sea, evaporates from the sea and land into clouds, and falls as rain back into rivers (the water cycle). Spring water is groundwater from rain that soaked into the ground. Day and night happen because the Earth spins. Tides happen because the pull of the Moon and Sun is a little different on each side of the Earth, so both sides bulge.

    Seasons happen because the Earth’s spin axis stays tilted 23.4 degrees as it goes around the Sun, which changes how high the Sun climbs and how long the days are. Some flat-earth maps show the Sun circling on the inside in summer and on the outside in winter, but that is not how the real Earth works. Animals of the same group really do tend to be bigger in colder places (Bergmann’s rule), though there are many exceptions.

    The idea that traits that help survival are passed to young and add up, so that living things change (natural selection), was published by Darwin and Wallace in 1858. Real evolution usually takes hundreds to tens of thousands of generations. In this garden it is made much faster, so you can see it in a few dozen generations.

    Long ago, many people thought the land was a flat disk. In the ancient Greek poems of Homer, a great river called Oceanus flows around the flat land, and Thales thought the land floated on water. In ancient Mesopotamia, people believed there was a sea of fresh water (the Abzu) under the land, and that the water of springs and rivers came from it. But waterfalls pouring off the edge of the world are mostly a picture from modern art and stories; few, if any, people long ago wrote about them. “Everyone in the Middle Ages believed the Earth was flat” is also a common myth. Educated people knew the Earth was round (even Ptolemy, who put the Earth at the center of the universe, thought it was round).

    Real tides also grow bigger around the new and full moon (spring tides), because the pulls of the Moon and Sun line up. Many animals are active at set times of day; wolves often hunt from evening into the night. Some living things, like grass puffers and corals, really do lay eggs in time with the Moon’s phases. Predators catch slow and weak prey more easily, and this is one of the forces that makes prey evolve better ways to escape.

    When groups of one species are cut off from each other by mountains or seas for a long time, each changes to fit its own place, until they become separate species that no longer have young together even if they meet (speciation by geographic isolation). In reality this usually takes thousands to tens of thousands of years. This garden makes it much faster.

    Dead living things and droppings are broken down by tiny living things (decomposers) into carbon, nitrogen and more, which become parts of plants again. The stuff bodies are made of goes around like this (the cycle of matter). But lives turning into light, riding the clouds and returning to a jar is make-believe in this garden. Genes are passed only from parents to young.
